# Batch 0 of the Docling parser: what runs, how long, and what stops the next batches

**Batch 0** is the first run of the `docling` parser over real EMA PDFs. It writes files
only, under `config.PARSED_ROOT` (`~/Nextcloud/Datasets/ema_nlp/parsed`). It writes no Mongo
row and changes nothing in the graph. How the parser works: [`docs/PARSING_DOCLING.md`](../../PARSING_DOCLING.md).

## The owner's yes, and the condition

On 2026-11-25 the owner said yes to batch 0 (relayed by the lead session): "about 270 PDFs,
7 to 12 GPU hours, files only". The plan adds the condition: the batch starts only if the
estimate stays inside that, and it is cut to fit otherwise. **The estimate for all 270 is
26 hours, so the batch is cut to fit 12 hours** (below).

## The rate, from 3 documents

On 2026-11-26 the batch command parsed 3 of the 270 in the parse environment
(`batch0/rate3.jsonl`, 250 W cap, one worker): 30 pages, 0 failures, 414.6 s in all. Docling's
stage timings (`settings.debug.profile_pipeline_timings`) show where the time goes:

| Document | Pages | Tables, cells | Seconds | Where the time went |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| pandemrix-h-c-832-ii-0034 assessment report (ranked, score 6,289) | 19 | 44, 4,982 | 305.1 | table stage 289.4 s (**0.058 s per cell**), OCR 16.2 s (0.85 s per page) |
| entresto paediatric compliance statement (random) | 1 | 2, 23 | 3.3 | |
| speakers-biography (random) | 10 | 0, 0 | 104.3 | **OCR 108.1 s**: 36 pictures (photographs); Docling OCRs bitmap regions on pages that do have a text layer, and RapidOCR runs on the CPU (`onnxruntime` has no CUDA provider) |

The exploration's estimate, 8.4 to 9.0 hours for 270, used 3.6 to 4.05 s per page. That
rate holds for ordinary pages. It does not hold for the ranked documents, which hold 4,962 to
10,975 table cells in at most 60 pages each.

## The estimate, with its arithmetic

- **Ranked**, per document: 0.058 s × cells + 0.85 s × pages. The cells are taken as the
  legacy score (the cells in complex tables). For the measured document Docling found 0.79 times
  the score, so this side is high. For the 200: 0.058 × 1,326,289 + 0.85 × 7,749 = 83,700 s =
  **23.2 h** (18.7 h with the 0.79 ratio).
- **Random**, per document: 9.78 s × pages + 0.058 s × score. 9.78 s per page is the mean of
  the two random documents, (3.26 + 104.3) / 11. It is high: the photographs dominate it. For
  the 70: 9.78 × 890 + 0.058 × 7,672 = 9,150 s = **2.54 h**.
- Overhead: a pause of 60 s every 20 documents and a model load of about 40 s for each new
  worker (one per 50 documents). That is 0.13 h for 126 documents.
- **All 270: about 26 hours. That is outside the owner's 12.**

**The cut:** the batch runs the 70 random documents, then the ranked ones in rank order. A
timer creates the stop file **11 h 00 min** after the start, and the batch ends after the
document in hand. The longest ranked document is estimated at 11.5 min (0.058 × 10,975 + 0.85
× 60 = 688 s). By the estimate, the 70 random and the first **56 ranked** documents finish:
126 documents, 3,502 pages, 10.98 h. The 2 random documents of the rate run are already
parsed and are skipped. The ranked documents that do not finish are first in the next batch.

**The worker test** (TASK-015) runs after the batch, on the same 20 documents twice: once with
two worker processes and once with three. The 20 are the random documents with the fewest
pages: 33 pages, an estimated 5 min with one worker
(`batch0/workers20.jsonl`). Each run writes to its own folder under
`$EMA_RESULTS_DIR/docling/batch0/`, not to `PARSED_ROOT`. Each run has its own stop-file timer
at 20 min. With the batch, all GPU work stays within 12 hours: 11 h 00 min, plus 11.5 min for
the document in hand, plus 2 × 20 min.

## What stops the next batches

These thresholds are set before the batch runs. Batch 0's report (`scripts/report_docling_batch.py`) measures each one.
If one is crossed, no further batch starts until the owner has seen the report.

| Measure | Stops the next batches when |
|---|---|
| Tables with numbers in which the cells miss a number of the text layer inside the table's box, or add one the layer lacks | **more than 20%** of the checked tables. Footnote marks that the layer flattens (layer 12.51, cell 12.5[^1]) do not count |
| Numbers held, either direction (layer by cells, cells by layer) | **below 98%**. On 30 pages the lead session measured 797 of 799 (99.7%) |
| Greek symbols in Latin text held (μ included), page text | **below 98%**, with at least 20 symbols counted. Where a page has a text layer, Docling takes the page text from it |
| Greek symbols in Latin text held, tables | **below 90%**, with at least 20 symbols counted. The owner's requirement (2026-11-25, relayed by the lead session): **"Greek symbols should be conserved."** |
| Failed or partial documents | more than 5% of the documents parsed |
| CJK characters | any document with more than 10. The corpus holds no Chinese, Japanese or Korean |

With fewer than 20 Greek symbols counted, the report gives the numbers and no stop decision is
made.

## Found on the way, for the checkpoint (TASK-017)

At these rates the whole corpus takes between 16 days (0.79 × 0.058 s per score cell + 0.85 s
per page) and 113 days (every page at 9.78 s) of one process. The proposal said 25 to 46. Two
things would shorten it, and both change the options hash:

- OCR on the GPU (`onnxruntime-gpu`).
- No OCR of pictures on pages that have a text layer.

Batching the table model's tables would shorten it too. Batch 0's report gives the rates on
about 126 documents instead of 3.
